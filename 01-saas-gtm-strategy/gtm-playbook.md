# B2B SaaS GTM Playbook

A 7-stage framework I use to plan and run go-to-market for B2B SaaS, Healthcare IT and IT services offerings.

```
1. Market & ICP  →  2. Positioning  →  3. Offer & Pricing  →  4. Channel Mix
       →  5. Sales Enablement  →  6. Launch  →  7. Measure & Optimize
```

---

## 1. Market & ICP Definition

**Goal:** Know exactly who we sell to and why they buy now.

- Segment the market by vertical, company size, region and buying trigger (regulation, funding, technology change, contract renewal).
- Define the **ICP** at account level (firmographics, technographics, triggers) and the **buying committee** at person level (economic buyer, champion, technical evaluator, procurement, blockers).
- Build a tiered named-account list:
  - **Tier 1:** 1:1 ABM
  - **Tier 2:** 1:few, by cluster
  - **Tier 3:** 1:many programmatic
- For public-sector / Federal markets, map the procurement path, contract vehicles and event calendar early. These set the timeline.

**Output:** ICP document, buying-committee map, tiered account list → [ICP template](templates/icp-template.md)

## 2. Positioning & Messaging

**Goal:** One clear reason to choose us, said the same way everywhere.

- Competitive alternatives (including "do nothing" and "build in-house")
- Unique capabilities → the value they create → proof points
- Messaging tailored to each buyer persona: a CIO, a CFO and a clinical or operations leader each hear the value in their own terms
- Brand architecture across the product portfolio (master brand vs. product brands)

**Output:** Positioning canvas, message house, proof-point deck → [Positioning canvas](templates/positioning-canvas.md)

## 3. Offer, Packaging & Commercial Model

- Packaging aligned to the buyer's maturity (pilot → department → enterprise)
- Pricing metric that grows with the customer's value
- Where it fits, **outcome-based revenue-share or gain-share** models, so the buyer pays partly on results
- Clear entry offer (assessment, pilot, proof of concept) to shorten the first decision

## 4. Channel Mix

| Channel | Best for | Typical role |
|---|---|---|
| ABM (LinkedIn, display, direct mail, executive outreach) | Tier 1–2 enterprise accounts | Create and accelerate opportunities |
| Industry events & forums (e.g. CHIME, NASSCOM, CII) | Healthcare, Federal, enterprise | Credibility, executive meetings |
| Inside sales (email + warm calling) | Mid-market & enterprise | Turn engagement into qualified meetings |
| SEO / content / CRO | Inbound demand | Low-CAC, compounding pipeline |
| Paid (LinkedIn, Meta, search) | Targeted reach & retargeting | Scale proven messages |
| Partners & alliances | New regions or verticals | Borrowed trust & reach |

Budget follows **pipeline per dollar** by channel (see [02 — Marketing Analytics](../02-marketing-analytics/)).

## 5. Sales Enablement

- Proof-point decks, ROI calculators, battlecards, objection handling
- Agreed lead definitions (MQL → SAL → SQL) and SLA for follow-up
- Account research briefs delivered to sellers automatically (see [03 — AI Marketing](../03-ai-marketing/))
- Joint weekly pipeline review: Marketing, Sales and Product

## 6. Launch

A phased 90-day plan covering internal readiness, soft launch to design partners, then full market launch → [Launch checklist](templates/launch-checklist.md)

## 7. Measure & Optimize

| Layer | KPIs |
|---|---|
| Executive | Marketing-sourced & influenced pipeline, win rate, CAC, CAC payback |
| Funnel | MQL → SQL → Opportunity → Won conversion, velocity per stage |
| Program | Cost per SQL, pipeline per $ by channel, account engagement score |
| Efficiency | Campaign launch time, research hours saved, cost per process |

Monthly review → quarterly re-allocation of budget and focus.
